The Book of Coach Kenny

A smaller player, a playoff bench, and the reason I still believe the best coaching happens in the quiet seconds before a kid hops the boards.

By Coach Kenny · Halton Hills · August 2026 · 6 min read

I still remember the look on his face.

Playoffs. Tight building. A smaller player sitting on the bench beside me, eyes locked on the other team like they were a wall he could not see over. He was on a line with a bigger kid, the kind of teammate who makes the ice feel a little safer just by being out there, and still, in that moment, he was scared.

Not tired. Not confused about the breakout. Scared.

Every hockey coach has sat in that pocket of silence. The shift is coming. The kid is about to go over the boards. You get one or two sentences to decide what kind of coach you are going to be.

I did not give him a speech about toughness. I did not tell him to shake it off. I told him the truth: the bigger player had his back on the ice. He was not walking into that shift alone.

He hopped the boards. He had a great shift. He scored.

I still get that feeling when I think about it, the one that reminds you why you stay late, why you drive to the rink on a school night, why you keep showing up. That is the book I have been writing for years, one bench conversation at a time.

Who I am when the whistle blows

My name is Kenny. Around the rinks in the Halton Hills area, I am Coach Kenny.

I have been on the baseball diamond for seven years with the Halton Hills Eagles, and on the hockey bench for ten years in this same community. I have three kids in sports, which means I am a coach, a parent, and a student every single week. They keep me honest. They keep me learning.

Away from the rink I have spent a decade in leadership in the construction world, guiding young technicians who are just starting out. Different job site. Same lesson. People do not grow when they are afraid to make a mistake in front of you.

I am a family man. I see my parents a lot. I see my in-laws a lot. They are great for the kids, and they are part of why our house still feels like a team even when the season gets loud.

And yes, I am a nerd. I like coding. I like trying nerdy things. That is a big part of why Coaching Hockey Made Easy exists. I wanted a workspace that actually fits how coaches teach, not a generic sports tool with a hockey sticker on it.

The club that started with a few family games

Sports for Fitness began the way a lot of good things start in a small town: a family organizing a few games, then realizing the kids kept coming back.

We are going into our tenth year as a not-for-profit. What started as weekend fun in Halton Hills has become Sports for Fitness: Kids Fun Fitness in the summer, Kids Fun Skating in the winter, hockey mini camps, and hockey development work focused on edges, crossovers, speed, and confidence. Our five objectives have not changed: create a fun and active environment, keep programs affordable, support local organizations, promote an active lifestyle, and stay open to ideas.

If a kid wants to play, they should have a place on the team. That is the whole point.

The page I keep coming back to

Here is my philosophy, and I will not dress it up.

Kids cannot be afraid to make mistakes. If they are, they shut down. And a kid who shuts down will never hit their potential.

Let them miss. Let them bobble the puck. Let them take the extra stride and get caught. Then let them learn from it and grow.

Kids will not always be at their best. Neither will you. Neither will I. That is not a character flaw. That is being a human being who plays a hard game.

Repetition is what brings the floor up.

It is easy to play when you are at the ceiling. Everybody looks like a hockey player on the night the puck is glued to their stick. The work is raising the nights when they are not. The work is making the “off” game still look like your team.

That is why I cut film. That is why I send a clip instead of a lecture. That is why I built a tool that lets a coach mark up a shift, assign it, and see if the player actually watched it. Confidence is not a poster in the dressing room. Confidence is a kid who has seen the play, tried it, missed it, tried it again, and knows his coach is still in his corner.

Coach takeaway

Do not wait for a kid to feel ready. Give them a reason to believe someone has their back, then give them enough clean reps that their floor rises on the nights they are not at their ceiling.

What that scared kid taught me

The goal was nice. I will not pretend I do not love a playoff goal.

But the shift started before he scored. It started when a smaller player believed, for twenty seconds, that he was not alone.

That is the job. Not the systems binder. Not the perfect practice plan. The job is getting a kid from scared to ready, and then giving him enough clean reps that his floor rises when the ceiling is not available.

If you are a hockey coach, you already have stories like this. This blog is where I am going to keep telling mine, and the lessons I keep stealing from baseball, from job sites, from my own kids, and from the bench.

Welcome to the Book of Coach Kenny.
